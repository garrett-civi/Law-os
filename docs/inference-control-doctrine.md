Inference Control Doctrine

Overview

This document defines how inference is generated, constrained, and reviewed across the Law-OS system.

Inference is not free-form reasoning.

It is a regulated system resource that must satisfy:

coherence → support → legitimacy → reviewability

Inference Control Doctrine governs how facts become conclusions at each stage of adjudication.

⸻

I. Core Principle

Inference must be strong enough to support legal meaning,
but constrained enough to avoid speculation.


⸻

II. System Placement

Inference operates across five layers:

M₁ → M₂ → Z₂ → Z₃ → ZZ

Each layer applies a different type of control.

⸻

III. Stage-by-Stage Mapping

⸻

1. M₁ — Factfinding (Inference Generation)

function:
    construct factual meaning from evidence

Inference Types:
	•	circumstantial inference
	•	pattern inference
	•	temporal inference
	•	behavioral inference

Controls:

- consistency with evidence
- non-contradiction
- plausibility within real-world behavior
- pattern stability

Failure Mode:

speculation


⸻

2. M₂ — Doctrinal Mapping (Inference Structuring)

function:
    map facts onto legal elements

Inference Types:
	•	element satisfaction inference
	•	intent inference
	•	causation inference

Controls:

- element anchoring
- doctrinal fit
- legally recognized inference types

Failure Mode:

misclassification or doctrinal mismatch


⸻

3. Z₂ — Liability Validation (Inference Audit)

function:
    validate whether the inference supporting liability is legitimate

Inputs:

verdict_record
liability_record

Controls (Zb dominant):

- evidentiary sufficiency
- record support
- reasonable inference vs speculation
- coherence across fact and doctrine

Controls (Za bounded):

- absence of distortion or fabrication

Failure Mode:

insufficient evidence or unreasonable inference


⸻

4. Z₃ — Outcome Validation (Inference Proportionality)

function:
    evaluate whether the outcome justified by inference is legitimate

Controls (Za primary):

- proportionality
- fairness of burden
- alignment between harm and remedy

Controls (Zb constraints):

- legal authority
- enforceability

Failure Mode:

disproportionate or unjustified outcome


⸻

5. ZZ — Appellate Review (Inference Governance)

function:
    review whether inference was properly used across all stages

Standards of Review:

de novo           → legal inference correctness (M₂)
clear error       → factual inference (M₁)
abuse of discretion → outcome/inference management (Z₃)

Controls:

- correct application of inference rules
- adherence to evidentiary limits
- avoidance of speculative reasoning

Outputs:

affirm
reverse
remand
modify

Failure Modes:

legal error
clearly erroneous inference
abuse of discretion


⸻

IV. Cross-Stage Invariants

Inference must originate from evidence (M₁)

Inference must map to doctrine (M₂)

Inference must be supported by the record (Z₂)

Inference must justify outcome (Z₃)

Inference must survive appellate review (ZZ)


⸻

V. Relationship to Evidence Doctrine

Inference Control Doctrine aligns with:

FRE 401 → relevance (inference connection)

FRE 403 → exclusion of weak or misleading inference

FRE 901 → authentication (foundation for inference)

Circumstantial evidence doctrine → permissibility of inference


⸻

VI. Structural Insight

Inference is the bridge between fact and law.

Inference Control Doctrine regulates the strength,
direction, and legitimacy of that bridge.


⸻

VII. Final Compression

M₁ → generates inference

M₂ → structures inference

Z₂ → validates inference

Z₃ → evaluates consequence of inference

ZZ → governs inference across the system


⸻

One-Line Definition

Inference Control Doctrine governs how facts become legally meaningful conclusions and ensures those conclusions remain legitimate across all stages of adjudication.
