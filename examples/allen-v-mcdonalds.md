Example — Allen v. McDonald’s (Plausibility Density via Public Data Injection)

Overview

This example demonstrates how a claim can survive plausibility at the L (recognition) stage through the use of:
	•	Public Narrative Embedding (PNE)
	•	Public Record Data Injection (PRDi)

This pattern shows how facts that would normally emerge during discovery can be introduced at entry, increasing plausibility without relying on a specific cause of action.

⸻

I. Core Insight

Public narratives + publicly available data
can increase factual density at L,
allowing claims to survive without discovery-dependent facts.

This is not dependent on civil rights doctrine.

It is dependent on fact density and inferential coherence at the recognition stage.

⸻

II. Factual Structure

Allegation (simplified)

A plaintiff alleges that McDonald’s:
	•	publicly promoted commitments to diversity, equity, and inclusion
	•	made public representations about supporting Black-owned media and businesses
	•	failed to allocate advertising or economic opportunity consistent with those representations

⸻

III. Public Narrative Inputs (PNE)

These are statements already made by the defendant:
	•	DEI commitments in public communications
	•	corporate diversity pledges
	•	public-facing branding around inclusion
	•	statements to investors, media, or the public

These inputs function as:

self-adopted narrative frames


⸻

IV. Public Record Data Inputs (PRDi)

These are independently verifiable facts:
	•	advertising spend data
	•	vendor allocation patterns
	•	publicly reported partnership distributions
	•	industry-level comparison data

These inputs function as:

externally verifiable factual anchors


⸻

V. Injection at L (Recognition Stage)

Normally:

complaint → limited facts → discovery → fuller factual development

With PDIM:

complaint + public narrative + public data
→ increased factual density at L

Effect:

facts that would normally appear in discovery
are introduced at the plausibility stage


⸻

VI. Mechanism of Plausibility Density

The interaction between PNE and PRDi creates:

1. Narrative Anchoring

Defendant has already defined the expected conduct.

2. Measurable Comparison

Public data allows comparison between stated commitments and actual behavior.

3. Reduced Speculation

Inference is grounded in:
- defendant’s own statements
- externally verifiable data

4. Increased Coherence

The claim is not hypothetical.
It is structured and testable at entry.


⸻

VII. Effect on L (Legal Recognition)

At the L stage, the system evaluates:
	•	non-conclusory factuality
	•	reasonable inference of liability
	•	doctrinal operativity
	•	coherence of allegations

PDIM strengthens all four:

More facts → less speculation
More structure → stronger inference
More alignment → higher plausibility


⸻

VIII. Output Classification

This process operates entirely within:

USE_reasoning_plausibility → L

Type:

state_flip

No USAGE occurs.

No institutional artifact is created.

But the quality of entry is materially enhanced.

⸻

IX. Key Structural Insight

The case survives not because of the legal theory alone,
but because the factual record has already been partially constructed
through public narrative and public data.


⸻

X. Generalizable Pattern

This example illustrates a reusable structure:

1. Identify public narrative adopted by defendant
2. Identify public data reflecting actual behavior
3. Align narrative with measurable outcomes
4. Inject both into the complaint
5. Increase plausibility at L


⸻

XI. Final Compression

Public narrative defines expectation

Public data reveals behavior

Mismatch creates structured inference

Structured inference survives plausibility


⸻

One-Line Definition

PDIM allows plaintiffs to convert public narrative and public data
into immediate plausibility at the recognition stage,
reducing reliance on discovery.
